What's new

Pokémon Origin Jade is a journey through three regions in one save file: Kanto first, then Johto, then Hoenn. This page shows what works differently from the original games on the way.

On this page

  3. Rules of your own
  4. More differences
  5. State of the beta

Three regions, one journey

Every region has its own Badges, its own League and its own title. The credits only roll after the first title in Hoenn.

  1. Kanto: You start in Pallet Town. Kanto follows the maps of FireRed and LeafGreen and leads up to the Pokémon League.
  2. Johto: After the title in Kanto, Prof. Oak gives you the GS Ball at his lab in Pallet Town. With it the guard at the end of Route 22 lets you through, and Route 26 takes you to New Bark Town.
  3. Hoenn: After the title in Johto, Steven is waiting at Prof. Oak's lab and gives you the Hoenn Ticket. The ship leaves from Vermilion City and Olivine City.

The routes in detail are under Getting started & FAQ.

What plays differently

Fixed levels

Wild Pokémon and Trainers have fixed levels that rise from Kanto through Johto to Hoenn. Nothing depends on your team. More under Level & EXP system.

Experience by region

A battle gives a different amount of experience in each region: in Kanto 100% up to Lv. 20 and 71% above that, in Johto 22%, in Hoenn 35%. That way your team grows with the journey.

Exp. Share from the start

The Exp. Share is in your Bag, switched on, when you start, and can be turned off and on at any time. It shares experience out instead of multiplying it.

Field moves without teaching them

For a field move it is enough to have the HM in your Bag and a Pokémon in your party that could learn it – you do not have to teach it. You still need the region's go-ahead: the Badge in Kanto, and in Johto and Hoenn the permission you are given on the way (for 6 moves in Johto, 6 in Hoenn).

Pokégear and rematches

Mom gives you the Pokégear right at the start, together with the Running Shoes. It has a phone, a map and a radio. Trainers you beat register themselves – 60 in Kanto, 28 in Johto, 64 in Hoenn – and call for a rematch once you have 3 Badges in their region. The calls can be turned off in the options under “Match Calls”.

Gym Leader rematches

After the title in Kanto you can challenge the eight Kanto Gym Leaders again in their Gyms, as often as you like. Their strongest Pokémon is 1 to 4 levels above the reference level for your progress; after the title in Johto they bring a stronger team.

Day and night

The game runs on a clock: in the morning (06:00–10:00), during the day (10:00–18:00), in the evening (18:00–19:00) and at night (19:00–06:00) the light changes, and in many places different wild Pokémon appear.

Pokémon that follow you

The first Pokémon in your party can walk behind you. You set that in the options under “Follower”.

Ships between the regions

With the Hoenn Ticket you sail from Vermilion City and Olivine City to Hoenn. The first crossing ends on Route 104; later Slateport City and Lilycove City are on the timetable once you have been there. The Battle Frontier is added after the title in Hoenn, Birth Island with the Aurora Ticket.

Legendary Pokémon come back

A legendary Pokémon you knocked out is not lost: it returns after every League victory. If it fled, it is back after 3 changes of day. Only a caught one never returns.

Mom's savings

As in Gold and Silver, Mom can save money for you. While saving is on, she puts aside an extra quarter of the prize money after every Trainer battle you win – nothing is taken from you.

Handy items

Bill gives you the PKMN Box Link after the title in Kanto, the Oval Charm comes with the first Egg from a Day Care, and Prof. Oak has a reward for every complete regional Pokédex: Exp. Charm, Shiny Charm and Aurora Ticket.

Sounds of the past

The GB Player is in your Bag from the start. The game says about it: “A device that plays music from the old Pokémon games.”

Rules of your own

Before you start, the challenge menu lets you decide how you want to play – and much of it can be relaxed again later on a PC.

  • Basic rules: movepool, physical/special by move, the Fairy type, reusable TMs and more can each be set back to how the third generation did it.
  • Nuzlocke: built in at three levels, with extra rules you can set. The rules apply from the moment you get the Pokédex until the first title in Hoenn.
  • Randomizer: starters, wild Pokémon, Trainers, types, moves, abilities, evolutions, items and more can each be shuffled.
  • Difficulty and special rules: level cap, party limit, items in battle, the opponents' IVs and effort values, pricier shops, a single type, Mirror Mode.

Every entry with its text is listed under Settings & game modes.

More differences

  • The game has the Pokémon of the first three generations together with their later evolutions and pre-evolutions. There are no Mega Evolutions, Gigantamax forms or regional forms.
  • Pokémon of a region only show up once the story has reached that region.
  • No trading needed: every Pokémon and every item can be obtained in a single save file.
  • Whether a move is physical or special depends on the move, and the Fairy type exists. Both can be changed in the challenge menu.
  • You get the Running Shoes right at the start, and you can run indoors too.
  • Contests work as in Ruby and Sapphire: one rank per Contest Hall.
  • The Battle Frontier and the Trainer Hill are in the game.
  • There is no Vs. Seeker; rematches go through the Pokégear.

State of the beta

  • These pages describe Version 0.9.1 (Beta). A beta can have bugs.
  • Save files do not always carry over between beta versions. If a save no longer fits, the game says: “This save file is from an older version of the game. It cannot be loaded and will be overwritten when you save.”
  • Trades and link battles are untested. Linking with the official games or other hacks is not supported.
